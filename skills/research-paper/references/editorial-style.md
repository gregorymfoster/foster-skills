# Research-site editorial style

The site publishes research essays, not research notes. Accuracy and source
ownership are invisible foundations. The reader should encounter an argument,
not the machinery that verified it.

## Calibrate before drafting

The canonical posts live in `~/code/research/_posts/`:

- `2026-08-14-good-life-after-sudden-wealth.md`
- `2026-08-15-bigger-problems.md`
- `2026-08-15-choosing-a-second-mountain.md`
- `2026-08-18-ten-times-the-rate-of-scientific-discovery.md`

Read the full post closest to the new topic. Read the opening through the first
substantive section, plus one later analytical section, from two other posts.
Before writing, make a private voice brief covering:

1. the reader and the reason to care;
2. the opening mechanism, such as a contradiction, case, or crisp verdict;
3. how the writer distinguishes evidence from interpretation;
4. paragraph rhythm and the typical size of a genuine section;
5. how links and quotations enter the prose.

Use those observations as the style specification. Generic instructions such
as "neutral research prose" do not override the samples.

## House voice

- Start with the answer, tension, or named case. A numerical baseline belongs
  early only when it creates the tension; it should not delay the reason to
  care.
- Write as a researcher with judgment. First person is useful when weighing
  evidence, defining a target, reporting a failed search, or giving a verdict.
- Let sources do narrative work. A named person, experiment, institution, or
  surprising provenance trail is stronger than a paragraph of detached facts.
- Use precise detail and plain sentences. Vary paragraph length. Spend more
  time on the surprising or consequential parts and compress taxonomic setup.
- Cite the claim where it appears. Evidence labels belong in nearby prose or
  concise parentheticals when the class is not already obvious.
- Organize around turns in the argument. A heading earns its place when the
  reader's question changes, not whenever the source dossier changes category.
- Preserve uncertainty without letting disclaimers become the voice. State a
  limitation once, precisely, then continue the argument.

## Editorial rejection tests

A draft fails before humanization if any of these are true:

- multiple paragraphs begin with labels such as "Empirical finding," "Empirical
  inference," "Calculation," or "Traditional wisdom";
- the first 500 words could be pasted into a technical briefing without
  changing their purpose or stance;
- the material that motivated the request appears only after definitions,
  taxonomies, or a long baseline section;
- citations arrive mainly as multi-link bundles at paragraph endings instead
  of attaching to the claims they own;
- sections average only a few paragraphs because the dossier's categories were
  copied into the article;
- the conclusion mostly repeats the introduction rather than sharpening or
  complicating it;
- removing headings would expose a sequence of same-shaped paragraphs;
- the prose has no first-person research judgment, mixed verdict, memorable
  case, or other sign that a particular mind weighed the material.

Passing these tests does not prove the essay is good. The lead still compares
the full draft beside the closest canonical post and asks whether a regular
reader would voluntarily continue after the opening page.
