# Seeds

Seeds are what the lenses work on. Ideas that come from a team's own edges beat ideas pulled from a trend list, because the team already knows the buyer and can reach them. Write each seed as one line.

## Founder edges

For each founder, list what a stranger couldn't easily copy:

- **Seen up close.** Tools, workflows and workarounds they used or built at past jobs, especially internal systems at large or fast-growing companies. These feed the internal-tool lens.
- **Paid for.** Things their past teams spent money on that felt overpriced, clunky or held together with contractors and spreadsheets.
- **Networks.** Buyers they can call this week: a community, past colleagues, customers, a region, a language.
- **Operating skills.** What they can run that most founders can't: a remote services team, enterprise sales, a regulated process, a supply side.

Ask of each edge: which buyer trusts this founder more than a random startup? That buyer is a seed.

## Principles and filters

Every filter in `principles.md` becomes a constraint on generation, not just on scoring. A team whose filter is "sell to someone with a deadline" should start from deadlines (audits, renewals, regulations) and work backward to products. Heuristics become lenses to lean on. Red flags become patterns to avoid while generating.

## The brief

From the founder profile's Team section and the request: time commitment, funding preference, preferred buyer and sales motion, markets to aim at, markets to avoid. A side project should favor ideas that pay for themselves early. A team that wants every founder to own a side of the business should favor ideas with two clear halves (for example, product plus a services or sales operation).

## No-go list

Write it down before generating: markets the profile avoids, anything inside a founder's non-compete or conflict, and ideas already tried or dropped in past runs. In anything shared beyond the founders, describe constraints in general terms and never quote agreements.
